Design of Thermal Systems

Spring 2026 MEMS 3430, WashU

Design of Thermal Systems (MEMS 3430) was a senior-year mechanical engineering course at WashU on analyzing and designing thermo-fluid and energy systems, covering power generation, heat exchangers, and renewable energy sources. The semester built toward a full combined-cycle gas turbine (CCGT) design through three sequential design homeworks, each building on the previous one, followed by a final group project.

Design Homework 1 · Brayton Topping Cycle

Designed an open Brayton cycle to power a solar-tower plant in the Mojave Desert, using concentrated sunlight as the heat source. I optimized the compressor and turbine isentropic efficiency pairing and compression ratio to minimize the required solar heat input — landing on 101.78 MW at a compression ratio of 12 — then sized the heliostat field needed to supply it.

Design Homework 2 · Rankine Bottoming Cycle

Designed a closed Rankine cycle for the bottoming half of the same combined-cycle system, cooled with water drawn from the Colorado River. I optimized the turbine inlet pressure against a minimum allowable turbine exit quality (to avoid turbine-blade erosion from droplet impingement), reaching a Rankine cycle thermal efficiency of 29.2% and a combined topping-plus-bottoming efficiency of 51.48%.

Design Homework 3 · CCGT Heat Exchanger Design

Designed the heat exchanger that couples the two cycles: a shell-and-tube preheater, a fire-tube evaporator, and a counterflow superheater. I sized each section's geometry and heat-transfer area to move heat from the Brayton exhaust into the Rankine working fluid, completing the combined-cycle design started in DH1 and DH2.

Download report (PDF)

Final Project · Solar Stirling Cycle

For the final group project (with Thanh Lung, Nina Woodward, and Matthew Brouillet), we designed a solar dish-Stirling engine and battery storage system to power WashU's Urbauer machine shop. We sized the system against an estimated 50 kW peak shop load and modeled the seasonal difference in solar input between summer and winter. The system produced a surplus of about 368 kWh on a sunny day, enough to cover the roughly 204 kWh needed to run the shop off the battery bank after sunset, with about 156 kWh left over as a safety margin.
