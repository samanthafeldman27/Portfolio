Nanochip Enclosure, Dutt FloQuET Lab

2024 Research Assistant

In the Dutt FloQuET Lab (University of Maryland), a nanophotonic chip and microscope experimental setup was sensitive to small airflow fluctuations in the room, which showed up as noise in the metrology readings taken off the chip. I designed, built, and tested a magnetized enclosure in SolidWorks to shield the setup from that airflow, prototyping and laser-cutting the design to fit the chip and its optical hardware.

The enclosure was a two-piece box with an interlocking, finger-jointed base and lid that snapped together with embedded magnets for quick access between test runs, with slotted side openings for fiber and cable pass-through and a top slot for the microscope's optical access to the chip.

I'm a co-author on the resulting paper, where this equipment supported the experimental setup: Y. Shen, P.-Y. Hsieh, S. K. Sridhar, S. Feldman, Y.-C. Chang, T. A. Smith, and A. Dutt, "Strong nanophotonic quantum squeezing exceeding 3.5 dB in a foundry-compatible Kerr microresonator," Optica 12, 302-308 (2025).
